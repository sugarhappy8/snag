# AnotherLife v4.0.0

This release has six parts. Company markets, listed stocks and funds now react to breakthroughs, yours and your rivals'. AI labs and chip companies get benchmarks, chip lines and a market price to compete on. A new Device and Platform Company trade makes phones and computers with chips from its own fabs or a sister company, which takes the game to 56 trades. Actors, astronauts, models and musicians play on a shared careers kit with named rivals, contracts and era rules. Product companies get a Research & Operations screen with process R&D, patents, an R&D tax credit and a research team. Eighteen trades get an Operations Desk with their own levers and events. Saves from v3.0.0 load, and the save schema stays 2.

## Markets that react to breakthroughs

When something breakthrough-class happens, the company markets that real life would move now move together: demand, costs, valuation, venture funding and rivals. Listed stocks, index funds and your own listed company follow on the same clock.

**What starts a wave.** A breakthrough is recorded as a wave with a kind (Frontier AI, Electric cars, Fusion power and so on), a size, and two numbers: hype, what investors expect, and delivery, what has actually shipped and sold. Four things write waves.

- **Your Heavy R&D launches.** 19 catalogue breakthroughs in 11 trades start one, for example Autonomous Agent Platform (software), Fusion Pilot Plant (energy) and Solid-State Cell Line (battery). A launch counts only within ten years of the catalogue's first date for that breakthrough, so a late launch of an old technology is a follower, not a wave.
- **Deep-tech milestones.** One to three per deep-tech trade: a new AI architecture, error-corrected qubits and quantum advantage, a reusable booster, fusion net energy gain, an approved brain implant, air taxis. The first frontier model of an AI lab and an approved drug with a big peak count too. Routine programs never do.
- **Workshop firsts.** Beating history to market, and winning the future race with nine of its thirteen ideas. Four consumer novelties write nothing.
- **Rivals' breakthroughs.** From 2027, a rival can land a breakthrough in a trade where you have a company. You see it as news. None lands within three years of a wave that already covers the trade.

You can have at most two waves from your own companies a year, and the game keeps at most 64 live.

**11 real episodes.** The history before 2026 is told by a table, not by invented waves: the transistor (1947 to 1960), television (1946 to 1960), the microprocessor and home computers (1975 to 1985), mobile phones (1983 to 2000), smartphones (2007 to 2013), cloud computing (2006 to 2015), the solar and battery cost collapse (2010 to 2020), reusable rockets (2015 to 2022), the EV boom (2019 to 2022), mRNA vaccines (2020 to 2021) and GLP-1 drugs (2021 to 2024). They apply from the next year you play. The dot-com boom and crash, the 2020 digital shift and the AI boom were already market events and are not repeated.

**Hype versus delivery.** A wave starts with hype above delivery. Delivery follows what really shipped: adoption for a Heavy R&D launch, the lab's market share for a deep-tech milestone, and a seeded schedule with the odd stall year for Workshop and rival waves. Hype closes on delivery each year, faster when delivery is behind. If delivery lags, the premium unwinds, so a bubble bursts because the breakthrough did not deliver and never because a year arrived. A wave that is fully delivered and matched by hype for five years settles into a permanent lift for the trade, so a finished wave leaves a larger sector, not a cliff. A wave that has barely delivered after six years is dropped with no lasting effect.

**Who moves.** Each kind lists three roles of trades.

- **Core trades** sell the breakthrough: demand and value rise, costs fall.
- **Suppliers and complements** gain less. Chip companies and data centers gain from an AI wave.
- **Disrupted incumbents** lose demand and value as delivery arrives. Software, marketing and education are exposed to an AI wave, and energy to fusion.

A wave reaches its home market in full and other countries less. The combined effect is held to the same bounds as market events. It shows in company demand and costs, in company valuation, in venture term sheets and deep-tech valuations, in NPC and rival companies, in a small yearly feed to the national economy, and in antitrust scrutiny of the core trade. A deep-tech or rival wave that touches your companies posts one market news item. The Annual Report gets one wave line and the market overview up to three rows, such as "Frontier AI wave. Demand +12%, value +14%." A quoted company's line says price in place of value.

**Listed stocks, funds and your listed company.** From 2026, a stock's price is its engine path times a sector level that follows the wave. The level climbs while delivery catches up and unwinds only if delivery lags hype. 23 of the 35 stock sectors map to a trade. The other 12 (banks, insurance, mining, hotels, airlines, shipping, agriculture, property, supermarkets, fashion, cosmetics and leisure) have no wave term, and neither do penny stocks, property trusts, coins, gold, bonds or savings.

- A quote carries a tag such as "Frontier AI wave: sector +12%". The market line names the wave behind the index, and the financial news gets at most one line a year.
- Index funds, fund returns, single-stock and index option settlements and the Investing talent's gut feeling all read the same level. Option premiums are still priced without waves.
- A real listed company takes the sector level both ways. The level names a sector, never a firm.
- Real booms before 2026 are already in the stock tables, so history waves never touch a stock level.
- Your own listed company takes the wave through its valuation. Its price closes half the gap a year, so it lags its peers on purpose, and buying your own shares still pays the higher of price and valuation plus 3%.
- **One market clock.** From fiscal 2026 the listed company's market move is the engine's move for the year the Stocks screen shows, the same one funds use. Before 2026 it is unchanged.

**The insider rule.** If you are the founder or the CEO of the company whose launch starts a wave, and you bought related stocks, calls or index calls in the year before the launch or in its year, the press may find out. The case runs through the existing insider machinery: exposure, a regulator, frozen bets, held money and arrest. Related means a core or supply sector of that wave. The three largest stakes open at most three cases, and gains under 1,000 in local money open none. A hired CEO or a plain owner is not an insider. It covers Heavy R&D launches, deep-tech milestones, the AI firsts and chip leaps of your own lab, and not Workshop or rival waves. Where you start the work, the Heavy R&D confirm and the deep-tech program confirm say: "If it works, related stocks move. Buying them in the year before the launch can count as insider trading."

## AI labs and chips

**AI Lab 2.0.** A model is now rated on eight capabilities: language, coding, vision, speech, reasoning and math, agents, robotics control and science. Each opens in its own year (language, vision and speech in 2012, reasoning 2014, robotics 2016, science 2017, coding 2021, agents 2022) and carries a weight in the overall score.

- **Benchmarks screen.** In the lab, Benchmarks shows your best released model per capability against the market: score, market average, best lab and your rank, for example "Coding 71. Market 64, best 78, rank 3 of 9." Tests get harder near the top, so a score can fall while your model does not change, and the screen says so. Real labs on the board carry a "(modeled)" tag.
- **Rival labs by year.** The labs on the world AI market have a rating per capability that rises with the real history to 2026 and with a seeded drift after it. Different labs lead different capabilities. Rivals follow a release: two years after it (one for open weights) the best rival in a capability is within 70 rating points of your best released model. A lead is earned and then fades.
- **Research.** Each capability has a research track under Benchmarks. It costs money for two years with fixed odds, and each level lifts every model you train after it. A lab can run one track for every three program slots, and at least one. A hired manager never starts a track. A track needs a trained model that covers the capability.
- **Demand follows the lead.** Demand and the enterprise pitch read your lead over the best rival, not a calendar line. A model is rated at most 90 points above the market's best in a capability that year, so a lead cannot compound.
- **Release and hold.** Holding a model back now stores it. A held model earns nothing and claims nothing until you use "Release" in Compute & Models.
- **Firsts.** The first lab to pass one of three milestones in a capability records the first for the family, which is 24 firsts at most. It shows in the news as a wave and lifts hype, brand and premium a little. An heir cannot claim the same first again, and the market can pass a milestone before you do.

**Silicon 2.0.** Chip companies sell more than one thing now.

- **One market price.** The Annual Report note says what chips like yours sell for, and Chips & Fabs, from the second commercial stage, shows your price as a multiple of that market price, for example 0.88x, against the rivals'. The Annual Report also shows "Goodwill · price vs market".
- **Chip lines.** General chips are sold at the headline price. Four more lines each take a discount of 0 to 50 percent below it, set in steps of 5: consumer graphics (from 1995), data-center CPUs (from 2000), memory (from 1970, needs your own fab) and data-center accelerators (from 2023, needs the AI-chip program). A line can only be cheaper than the headline, so a price promise holds on every line. Chips & Fabs also ranks your performance, quality, price and segment share against rivals.
- **The 2024 to 2027 squeeze.** Chip supply is tight in those years, strongest in 2025, strongest in the United States and weaker abroad. Street prices run above list on the scarce lines.
- **Goodwill.** A price below the market on the scarce lines wins goodwill (0 to 100, 50 neutral) in the squeeze, but only as far as you supplied it. A price you cannot supply earns almost nothing, and a price below cost earns nothing extra. Outside the squeeze goodwill relaxes toward 50. It moves your brand slowly, with a dead zone and a cap, and it never moves hype. The screen names the band: angry, cool, neutral, warm, loyal or devoted.
- **Rival price reactions.** If you undercut by at least 8 percent and supplied at least half of demand, the largest rival may cut prices to match for two years. It is less likely in the squeeze.
- **Launch choice.** When an AI-chip or a process-node win lands in a squeeze year and you run the lab yourself, "Price the new chips?" offers Undercut the market, Match the market or Keep my prices. A hired CEO follows the Pricing Policy row instead, a cap on the headline price as a share of the market. The Business talent's hint counts profit only and says it does not count goodwill.
- **News.** A chip company whose chips beat every rival on performance makes the news ("Record-speed chips") once, and again only after three years without the lead.

## Device and platform companies

**The trade.** Device and Platform Company is a world-market trade. Found one from 1977 in the United States, later where home computers arrive later. It sells eight product families from their first years: Home Computer (1977), Device Operating System (1981), Mobile Phone (1983), Desktop PC (1985), Laptop (1995), Smartphone (2007), VR Headset (2016) and AR Glasses (2019). Before the modern kinds reach the market it develops four period ideas: Pocket Calculators, Digital Watches, Cordless Telephones and Handheld Game Units. It has five Heavy R&D breakthroughs: Pen-Input Pocket Computer (1993), Pocket Music Player (2001), Touch Slate Computer (2010), Foldable Display Phone (2019) and Spatial Headset Computer (2024). 24 named device makers, 19 of them still trading in 2026, share the market with a procedural tail. The home computer and smartphone history waves list device makers as core trades.

**Spec sheets.** On the company screen, after Products, Devices & Chips shows each hardware family you sell as three rows against the rivals that sell the same kind that year: Performance, Quality and Price, for example "Performance 68. Market 67, best 74, rank 6 of 12." The rival figures are modeled and no row names a rival. Price ranks the cheapest first. Performance reaches demand only through a quality lift from your chips, and the screen says so.

**Chips.** Chips are 35 percent of a device's unit cost. Three sources are possible, and only the best one applies, so they never stack.

- **Open-market chips** are the default, at the mainstream node and a 70 percent yield.
- **Your own fabs.** Build a Chip Fab, up to three. The first costs about 150 million dollars at the US price level, the second 35 percent more and the third 70 percent more than the first, and the fabs carry a yearly upkeep of 7 percent of what they cost. One, two and three fabs cut the chip share of unit cost by 15, 25 and 30 percent, and the better chips raise performance, which lifts quality by up to 6 points. The cost is sunk and a fab cannot be sold. A hired CEO never builds one. The row shows what the fab saved last year.
- **A sister fab.** Assign a semiconductor company in the same group as the chip supplier under Group Services. It needs the same country, a group that owns all of both companies (a funded chip company must be bought out first) and a sister that is already shipping chips. The cut depends on the sister's stage, 8, 18 or 24 percent for fabless, own fab and leading edge, times how much of the buyer's chip bill the sister can cover from a reserve of a fifth of its own revenue. While it supplies chips the sister holds that capacity back from outside customers, and the buyer gives up its cross-selling lift. The saving appears with the sister's name. If your own fabs are the better source, the contract does nothing.

**Operating system.** A Device Operating System line makes customers stick. Its pull on the installed base is bounded and rises with the quality of the line, it ages without a refresh, and a better rival can still take customers.

## Careers

**The shared kit.** Actor, Astronaut, Model and Musician run on one kit. Each keeps one optional record, stamped with its owner, so an heir starts clean and meets the same named rivals. Rivals are fictional, named by country, era and five-year cohort, and an era or country with no field has no peers. Each career year closes once, with a row in a report card: your rank among peers, the last five years, top peers and the rivals who remember you. A career year offers at most one choice and two notices. Crisis choices carry odds, and with the matching talent (Acting, Modeling, Academics or Music) the hint states the option's own odds and marks the best one. Money moves only through audited earnings and payments. No new screen and no new minigame.

**Actor.**

- **Industries by country.** Hollywood (United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand), Bollywood (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh), Nollywood (Nigeria, Ghana, from 1992), K-drama (Korea), Hong Kong, and a domestic industry elsewhere. Each has its own film start, its scale, a yearly heat and its own invented awards, for example Golden Reel (1929), Primetime Glow (1949), Silver Lotus (1954), Jade Crane (1963), Lantern (1982) and Palm Screen (2005).
- **Eras.** Hollywood runs a studio system from 1928 to 1955 with forced terms. Streaming roles need streaming technology, so there is no streaming role in 1960.
- **Pipeline.** Auditions land a role that becomes a film or TV project with a budget tier (indie, studio, blockbuster, cable, network, streaming). A landed role offers a contract: Sign as offered, Take points with a lower fee, Sign exclusive or Push for more. A project can be developed, given co-stars, a press tour, an awards window, a clash dodge or an awards campaign, or walked away from.
- **Your team.** An agent and a manager, with cuts and loyalty, who can be poached. A press agent, range work against typecasting and a scandal menu (Apologize, Crisis PR, Lie Low).
- **Rivals.** Named rivals compete for roles by type and for nominations, and a loss to one names them and builds a grudge.
- **Eleven career events**, among them the move to sound films, guild strikes, a franchise offer, a shelved film, a censor and a rival feud.
- **Likeness trust.** Create a Likeness Trust to move residuals into it, then License for Five Years, Promote the Estate or Sell All Rights. It is an asset, so an heir inherits it.

**Astronaut.**

- **Era and access.** The Academy opens in 1959 and is closed before, with the reason shown. Crewed flights start in 1961 for the United States and Russia, 2003 for China, 1983 for partner agencies, with a private operator class from 2008 and commercial crew seats from 2020. Agencies carry their real names only from their own years.
- **Selection board.** In a class year a graduate sits a board: "You placed 9th of 62. The class took 14." The numbers depend on the agency and the era. It replaces the old flat chance of an offer. Applying is one try a year, and the hint says what the class wants.
- **Flight Board.** Up to four offers a year (type, days, seat, risk, your edge over a named rival), up to two bids, then a tour length and a regimen. Flights cost bone, radiation dose and family strain, and a radiation dose of 100 bans flights. The agency's funding and mood follow the era and the space budget. A lost crew opens an inquiry that asks for testimony, and a medical board can ground you.
- **Exits.** Speaker (lectures), author (a memoir with a tail of payments), administrator (Deputy Administrator and Administrator, to age 68), commercial flyer (Fly Your Own Capsule, if you own a space company with a crew capsule) and politician (a small poll edge). A Corps Record page shows legacy points, and retiring at 60 or later earns a Hall of Fame notice.

**Model.**

- **Season Board.** It replaces the old book. You get 3 castings a year plus half your title level, rounded down, across couture, autumn, spring and year-round seasons, with day caps of 8, 12, 12 and 24. Odds come from your fit against named rivals shortlisted for the same job.
- **Circuits and agencies.** Paris couture opens in 1900, New York 1943, Milan 1958, Paris ready-to-wear 1966, London 1984, Tokyo 1985, Sao Paulo 1996 and Berlin 2007. Four fictional agency tiers start in 1925 (Hometown Faces, Silhouette Models and later Prestige Model Management and Icon Elite). Before 1925 only freelance work runs.
- **Contracts.** A Guarantee contract pays a floor with exclusivity, and a commission-only contract has no floor. Each fee splits into agency commission, a mother agency's share, advance recoupment and your net, to the dollar. Renewal offers Renew as is, Push for terms or Walk away, and you can Switch Agency.
- **Strain and direction.** Workload builds strain. Rest and Recovery lowers it. Direction picks runway, editorial or commercial work, and each has its own prime years. Copy is about workload only.
- **Exits.** Become a Photographer, Launch a Fashion Label (a real company in the fashion trade) or Open an Agent Desk and scout proteges. Ranking is against fictional rivals and rank 1 is Model of the Year.

**Musician.**

- **Format eras.** Shellac, vinyl, tape, CD, download and streaming by year and country, which change the unit price, costs, the artist's take and how long a release keeps selling. Poorer countries lag the United States.
- **Label deals.** Labels are invented, in four tiers (for example Garage Door Records, Echo Chamber Records, Velvet Records and Meridian Sound Group) with a local floor label in every country and year. A deal offer asks you to Sign the deal, Push for royalty (odds shown with the talent) or Walk away. The advance is paid yearly and recouped from royalties, then a renewal screen opens at expiry with a push for more. Leaving forgives what is unrecouped and never makes a debt for you or an heir. The Label & Contract screen shows all of it.
- **Charts.** A release charts against named rival acts by era and country. Genres open by year (Rock 1954, Reggae 1964, Rap 1979, Alternative 1980) and so do subgenres, from Rockabilly in 1954 to Mumble Rap in 2014. Charts & Finances shows the top 10, the format mix, your last statement and your releases.
- **Release Plan.** Write it fresh, Clear every sample or Skip clearances. Skipping carries a claim risk if the release charts, and the claim becomes a settle, fight or credit-them dispute with a named claimant.
- **Touring.** Club, theater, arena (from 1955) and stadium (from 1965) tours have real seats, shows, crew, travel and production costs. A tour can lose money, and the screen shows a break-even per night with the talent. Tours are blocked in 2020 and 2021 and in war years.
- **Events and awards.** Collaboration, award, festival, scandal, label clash and royalty audit events. Each country has an invented ceremony, for example the Golden Record Award in the United States from 1959 and Naija Sound in Nigeria from 2000.
- **Master buy-back.** A release two years old or more can be bought back from the label for a master asset that an heir can inherit.

## R&D for every company

R&D 3.0 fills the space between a product line and a breakthrough.

- **Tiers.** Every trade has a tier that sets how far its R&D reaches: low (21 trades), mid (13), high (10, device companies among them) and breakthrough (the 12 deep-tech trades).
- **Research & Operations.** Product companies get this screen on the company screen, after Products. Deep-tech, finance and game-studio companies do not, because each already has its own R&D.
- **Operations R&D.** Nine programs in three tracks, each with three rungs and a first year: unit cost (Time-and-Motion Study 1905, Lean Production System 1955, Computerized Planning 1985), output (Layout Redesign 1905, Process Automation 1960, Robotics and Sensors 1990) and quality (Inspection Checklists 1905, Statistical Process Control 1950, Automated Quality Tracking 1995). Price and years are fixed per trade and rung and never scale with your cash. The row shows price, years, odds and the ceiling. Low trades stop at rung 2. Effects have a ceiling by tier (unit cost down 5, 8 or 10 percent for low, mid and high), fade 5 percent a year, and each win on a track is worth 20 percent less than the last. One program runs per track, with a three-year wait after a win.
- **Patents and licensing.** Patent a line you developed and launched in the last three years when no rival sells one yet: for a number of years rivals cannot sell one, 3 for low trades, 6 for mid and 5 for high before the country's enforcement. Low trades can patent only Heavy R&D breakthroughs. You can file a family once. You can license a held patent, which cannot be undone and ends the exclusivity, to earn royalties only from rivals that really sell the kind, up to 1.5 times the development price the patent is based on. A patent adds a little to company value and fades.
- **Credits and grants.** The R&D tax credit uses the deep-tech table by country, at most half of the R&D that started that year and at most 250,000. Countries also pay research grants on Operations programs, one every three years, for companies of up to 25 sites. The Annual Report has "R&D tax credit and grants", "Patent licences" and "Research team" lines.
- **Research team.** Hire a Scientist and Let a Scientist Go. 2, 4 or 6 scientists for low, mid and high trades, a three-year minimum service, a hire fee and a yearly wage. They raise the odds of every R&D project and add project slots.
- **Rival R&D.** Rivals now improve their versions of each kind the longer they sell it, up to 8 points over 12 years, so a line has to be renewed.
- **Hints and hired CEOs.** With the Business talent, rows say what a program, patent or scientist is worth on average. A hired CEO makes up to two such moves a year under a growth mandate and one under balanced or profit, and none under cash, inside the board's capex limits.

## Sector families

A shared framework gives trades their own systems without a second simulator: an Operations Desk on the company screen with a family name, four levers with a price and a trade-off, one story a year at most, a key role that moves one score, and a hired CEO that works the desk. Three families are built, covering 18 trades.

- **Food and Drink Service** covers restaurants, food trucks, cafes and bakeries. The desk is Kitchen and Floor: a hygiene grade, a review rating and the squeeze of peak hours. Levers: Menu style, Delivery, Opening hours and Kitchen audit. Eight events (inspection, review, platform fee hike, recall, festival, chef offer, noise, rush). The key role is a Food Safety Lead. Delivery shields part of a pandemic loss.
- **Care, Education and Personal Services** covers clinics, education, gyms, salons, pet care, home services and auto repair shops. The desk is Clients and Standards: a client base that repairs slowly, an accreditation tier and a payer mix. Levers: Recurring plans, Payer mix, Intake and Accreditation program. Seven events, including an accreditation review, a star staff member leaving and a payer audit. Each trade has its own key role, such as a Clinical Governance Lead or a Registrar.
- **Utilities, Land and Fleets** covers energy, solar, water, telecom, data centers, farms and logistics. The desk is Plant and Network: plant condition, outages, contract mix and weather. Levers: Overhaul, Contract mix, Fuel hedge and Weather cover. Seven events: storm, rate case, fuel spike, subsidy, big customer, shortage and recall.

The first fiscal year only measures. From the second year the company's numbers move with what the desk shows. Each lever changes once a year, a paid one books its cost at once, and the row says what it costs and what it gives up. A purple hint marks the best choice from the odds the resolver rolls. A hired CEO above skill 75 works the desk, changing at most two levers a year and reporting it in the yearly letter. A weaker CEO leaves the levers alone, and the desk says so. Clinic, education, pet care, home services and bakery companies show a rough estimate line while the 2020 crash is active. Weather, spot prices and outages are fixed by seeded draws, so reloading re-rolls nothing.

## Old saves

New state is optional and validated. Saves from v3.0.0 load and `SAVE_SCHEMA` stays 2. Nothing is paid or refunded on load.

- **Waves.** A save with no wave record has no waves. History waves apply from the next year played. Settled sector factors and live waves pass to an heir. Insider cases do not, because they belong to the person.
- **Stocks and listed companies.** Share prices before 2026 are unchanged. From 2026 a listed company's price uses the market clock above, so its path differs from v3.0.0.
- **Deep-tech companies.** `TECH_VERSION` goes from 3 to 4, so each deep-tech company is re-normalized once when first read. An AI lab's earlier models keep their single score and are priced by the old frontier line until you train a rated model. Years before 2012 use the old formulas. Chip companies store nothing new until a squeeze year, a lane discount or a pricing policy, so every year before 2024 books as before, and a company that never sets a lane discount sells as before. The Annual Report's bargain-price tip now reads "Bargain pricing. More buyers, thinner margins." for every trade.
- **Companies.** The 18 sector-family trades each get one more key role, which can raise the number of key staff a small multi-site company holds. Food companies use the family's inspection and review events in place of the generic ones once the desk is live, and these trades see more events a year. Their random stream differs from v3.0.0 from the second year. Lines of 14 trades now age faster: telecom, data centers, battery, solar and online banks lose quality at 2 points a year, up from 0.7, and auto repair, construction, logistics, energy, water, recycling, packaging, furniture and clinics at 1.2. Rivals' versions rise slowly everywhere, by hash and with no random draw. The R&D tax credit starts with the first fiscal year booked after the update, for projects started in that year.
- **Actors.** A life with a legacy actor profile is lifted in memory. Credits, awards, reputation, the agent and the project in progress carry over. The first yearly tick after the update adopts the old data, and the old rules run for that year. Residuals from credits now decay, and roles that did not fit the era, such as streaming roles in 1960, stop appearing.
- **Astronauts.** A serving astronaut gets a corps record at the next age-up from the old counters and performance, with no retroactive damage. Bone and radiation start fresh. Promotions now need standing and a free seat (12, 8, 4 and 1 seats for ranks 2 to 5), administrators retire at 68, and graduating cadets and applicants sit the selection board in place of the old chance. The Academy is closed before 1959.
- **Models.** A model's retainer is read as a Guarantee contract, and a legacy job pays exactly its salary when nothing is booked. The old book row is replaced by the Season Board, and agencies, circuits and offer kinds follow the year.
- **Musicians with a label deal.** They are grandfathered on the old terms for the rest of the current term. The advance keeps paying yearly, each release pays the old one dollar a copy with the old sales, and nothing is recouped. The Label & Contract screen shows "legacy terms". At expiry the renewal opens on the new screen with nothing owed and a royalty a little above the tier's base, so the first new deal is not worse than a fresh one. Old releases keep the old royalty tail.
- **Bands and unsigned musicians.** They have no deal to grandfather. New releases pay under the format-era waterfall at about parity with the old dollar a copy, and tours become a real profit and loss, so they pay much less than the old free tour money.
- **Heirs.** Career records belong to the person. An actor's likeness trust and a musician's masters are assets, so they inherit.

## Details

**Waves.** `business/tech-wave-data.ts` holds the 23 wave kinds (12 deep-tech, 11 history) with their core, supply and disrupted trades, and the `HEAVY_WAVES` (19), `DEEPTECH_WAVES` and `WORKSHOP_WAVES` tables and `INSIDER_WARNING`. `business/tech-wave-factors.ts` is the pure reader leaf (`waveFactors`, `STOCK_BETA` 1.5), `business/tech-waves.ts` writes and steps waves (`announceWave`, `advanceTechWaves`, `landRivalWaves`, `waveLines`) and `history/tech-waves.ts` holds the 11 `HISTORY_WAVES`. Flags are `g.flags.techWaves` (at most 64) and `techBase`, and `BizState.wave` is the valuation stamp. Stocks: `invest/wave-stocks.ts` (`SECTOR_TRADE`, `waveStockFactor`, `waveIndexFactor`, `waveTag`, `waveClause`, `waveNews`), `invest/clock.ts` (`marketYearOf`, `bookedMarketMove`), `business/wave-insider.ts` (`waveInsiderCases`) and `openInsiderCase` in `special/politics.ts`. The level folds into `shockFactor` and `indexShockFactor` in `invest/shocks.ts`.

**AI Lab 2.0.** `business/ai-bench-data.ts` (the eight capabilities, `CAP_IDS`), `ai-bench.ts` (ratings, `capMilestone`), `ai-bench-market.ts` (`labStanding`, rival board and catch-up), `ai-bench-claims.ts` (`claimFirsts`, flag `aiFirsts`), `deeptech-bench.ts` (screen lines), `scorecard.ts` (`scoreRow`, `scoreText`, shared with chips and devices). `AiModel` gains optional `rating` and `held`, and `TechState` gains `caps` and `firsts`. Capability tracks are `cap.<id>` programs in `PROGRAMS.aiLab` with a `track` field.

**Silicon 2.0.** `business/silicon-data.ts` (`CHIP_LANES`, `SHORTAGE_MARKUP`), `silicon.ts` (goodwill, `brandStep`, rival reaction, chip leap), `silicon-text.ts` (rows and bands), `priceIndex` and `marketReference` in `deeptech-core.ts`, `GPU_SHORTAGE` and `shortageIntensity` in `history/market-events.ts`, and the `deeptech.launch` script in `deeptech-sim.ts`. State is the optional `TechState.silicon`.

**Devices.** `business/devices-data.ts` (chip rules, `FAB_*`, `SISTER_CUT`, `OS_*`), `devices-specs.ts` and `devices-menu.ts` (the Devices & Chips screen), `market-data-devices.ts` (the 24 rival rows), `group-services.ts` (`chipBlock`, chip supply), `buildFabAction` in `manage.ts`. Optional `BizState.fabs` and `BizState.chips`.

**Sector families.** `business/sector-state.ts` (`FAMILY_TRADES`, `readSector`), `sector-core.ts` (the yearly hook), `sector-events.ts`, `sector-menu.ts` (`applyLever`), `sector-ceo.ts`, `sector-data.ts` (`SECTOR_ROLES`, history rows), and `sector-food*.ts`, `sector-care*.ts`, `sector-util*.ts`. Optional `BizState.sector`. Seeded multi-year tests of the 18 trades may need new baselines, because the event roll moves their random stream from the second year.

**Careers.** `special/kit/` holds `state`, `rivals`, `year`, `crisis`, `menu` and the `careers` registry, run by the `careers2` yearly hook in `special.ts`. Careers are `actor-*.ts`, `astronaut-*.ts`, `model-*.ts` and `music-*.ts` (`music-job`, `music-data`, `music-state`, `music-market`, `music-label`, `music-tour`, `music-events`, `music-menu`, `music-sim`, with `adoptMaster` in `music-rights.ts`). Flags are `special.actor.sim`, `special.astronaut.sim`, `special.model.sim` and `special.music.sim`. Legacy script ids and the legacy profiles stay.

**R&D 3.0.** `RD_TIER` and `rdTierOf` in `industries.ts`, `AGEING` in `products.ts`, `riseOf` in `product-market.ts`, and `business/lab-state.ts`, `lab-data.ts`, `lab-rules.ts`, `lab-year.ts`, `lab-menu.ts` and `lab-text.ts`. `bookLabYear` books credits, grants, royalties and payroll once a year, and `advanceLab` rolls programs. Optional `BizState.lab`. An id with no tier row reads mid.
