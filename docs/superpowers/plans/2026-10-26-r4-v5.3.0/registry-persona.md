## 1. Track summary

**Items covered (11 tasks).** D.1, the registry kernel: R4-S1, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S11. D.2, persona: R4-S2 (axes, trait bridge, voices), S9 (the player's persona), S10 (reader migration at parity).

**The design in one paragraph.** A person who is not yet in `g.people` is a **slot** `(kind, place, cohort, ordinal)` and its text form is the person's **sid** (`cls.us.2004.12`, `actor.us.1985.3`). `latent(familySeed, slot)` is a pure function of the family seed (the life id without `-g<n>`, so heirs, reloads and the Time Machine see the same person) and the slot, read from numbered facets of one `streamKey`, never from `g.rng` and never from mutable state. A person the game has met, or is bound to by a debt, a spouse, an heir, a company or a feud, is a **Met record** in the new shard `g.world.met`: a keyed object `sid -> record`, saved as an entity-sharded slice so a tap rewrites one chunk. A record stores the identity as it was resolved the day it was first met (name, gender, birth year, country, five persona axes), the year last seen and the stake flags, so a later edit to a name table never renames anyone. "One name is not two people" is settled at that moment only (`resolveName`: eight stored alternates, then a hyphenated surname). The shard keeps at most 400 records and 200 tombstones, evicts by [protected, salience, recency, key] so the survivors depend only on the set of names, and compresses a record that is idle but owed or feuding into a tombstone. Nothing exists until something touches it, and this track ships no writer in a played life, so no life, save or golden digest moves. The Person bridge is `materializePerson`: it calls `createPerson` with every option given, so it draws nothing from `g.rng`, and it stamps `data.sid` on a NEW person only (stamping an existing one renames its ledger account, `cash.ts:75-77`). `createPerson` keeps its signature and draw order. D.2: five axes (ambition, honesty, empathy, risk, steadiness), 0 to 100 and whole numbers in any stored tuple (the player's ambition is `ambitionOf`, which has two decimals); the 30 trait words and the 13 staff traits map to deltas around 50, words come back from axes, so a person made from a slot wears words every old reader understands; six narrator voices are a pure function of the axes; the player's persona is inferred (hidden stats, lifetime counters, `ambitionOf`) plus a bounded, owner-stamped nudge record in `flags['persona.me']`. The persona is stored but unwired in R4: nothing the player sees changes through D.2 (X18).

- **No live writer.** Nothing in a played life calls `touchMet` or `materializePerson` here. They register no protector (X13); L4 calls `touchMet` (with an identity) and `markMet` for the spouse and heir bits (X14), and nothing in the other three tracks calls `evictMet`, `tombstoneMet` or `sweepMet` directly (grep of the four plans).

## 3. Where the roadmap and the design are stale (and what the plan does)

- **Version.** R4 is v5.3.0; the roadmap was relabeled in 3f02589 and its R4 block starts at line 246.
- **"Met lives in `g.world.people`"** (design 7.2). That shard is an index of `{ id, seen }` rows over Persons that stay in `g.people`; its normalizer drops every other field (`world/people.ts:48-53`), and four places assume "people = the 80 intimate" (`lod.ts:127`, `invariants.ts:141-155`, `budget.ts:69-70`, `test/ceiling-kit.ts:452-453`). The plan adds the shard `met` and leaves `people` alone (X9). Records change on taps, so `met` is an entity-sharded slice, not a row of the `world` chunk (docs/SAVE.md:83: a shard that changes more than once a year does not belong there).
- **"`createPerson` becomes a wrapper that materializes a slot first, so the 55 call sites keep working."** 54 sites, not 55, and they draw `g.rng`; a wrapper moves every seeded life. The plan adds `materializePerson` and keeps `createPerson`.
- **"Ids stay per life; `sid` is stable across heirs and the Time Machine."** `Person.id` already survives heirs (`life.ts:1371` spreads the person, `nextId` is cloned) and a jump restores the whole snapshot. A sid adds value only for people who exist as slots, for one identity across shapes, and for re-meeting. It also carries a hazard the design misses: the ledger already names `person:<data.sid>` (`cash.ts:75-77`), so a sid on an existing person, or on a future heir (`life.ts:1313`), renames an account. Hence new persons only and the `heirUnsafe` invariant.
- **"`latent` is built on `seeded()`."** `docs/STREAMS.md` and the guard say world modules use named streams. `latent` uses `streamKey` and `streamUnit` (pure, no generator), so no stream-table row is needed and facets can be pinned.
- **"One name is not two people" as a property of `latent`.** Impossible: `rosterAt` picks a name against the earlier rivals of its build (`rivals.ts:148-171,193-194`), so uniqueness is order dependent. It is enforced at promotion only; two unpersisted slots may share a name.
- **"Eviction by salience (|affection| + resentment + obligation + role weight)."** Affection and resentment are the Edges track's. The kernel ranks by [protected, salience, recency, key] and takes salience from `registerSalience` (the edge part only, `f + s + abs(o)`), plus a role weight it stores (`w x 10`).
- **"Readers move over" to axes** (design 7.3). At parity only: a formula that changes its number moves every seeded life. The plan lifts tables and keys seeds by `personKey` (equal to `p.id` for every existing person) and lets a reader use an axis only for a sid-born person.
- **"The player's ambition is the Effort Engine's `A`."** True and kept: `inferAxes(g).ambition === ambitionOf(g)`; NPC `NpcLife.ambition` is a separate stored 5 to 98 number (`npc-lives.ts:104`, read as 50 by default at `npc-state.ts:19`).
- **"+20,000 nodes."** The shard is at most 10,401; the sum with the Edges track passes both the old 25,000 budget and the old 40,000 cap, so P2 raises the cap to 80,000 and the budget to 55,000 nodes and 800,000 bytes with an allotment of about 29,000 nodes for R4 (X47).
- **Line numbers.** `Person` is `types.ts:384` (roadmap 378); the hand-over is `life.ts:1367-1470`; `addRelationship` has 373 call sites, none in this track's hot files (grep, re-run in the second pass: 0 in `people.ts`, `npc-lives.ts`, `npc-state.ts`, `types.ts`, `will-core.ts`, `nepo-model.ts`, `nepo-succession.ts`, `nepo-job.ts`, `cash.ts`, `world/people.ts`, `world/lod.ts`, `world-flags.ts`), so the signature codemod does not touch them.

## 5. Risks

1. **A sid on an existing person renames its account** (`cash.ts:75-77`, `world/people.ts:31-33`) and splits the heir's history if it lands on a future heir (`life.ts:1313`). Guard: new persons only, `SID_RELATIONS`, `heirUnsafe`, a `metProblems` line, a hand-over test (S8), and S11's `sid:` scan.
2. **Silent loss at the node cap.** `normalizeWorld` drops the largest shard whole (`world/state.ts:153-160`); with edges and facts the registry may be it. P2 merges before S4 and fixes the drop order; S4's maximal-world test reads the constants.
3. **First production entity slice.** Unproven in the game; S5 spikes it and has a fallback (X9). E6b copies it.
4. **Latent facets are a promise.** Moving one renames every unmet person; golden file plus facet pin, recorded only after the lead has read `KIND_AGE` and `FACET`. Name tables change between releases; resolved identities are stored (tombstones keep names).
5. **Name order dependence** is unavoidable and stated (S6); a rival of `rosterAt` and a Met person may share a name until R6 D.6. The brand-collision list is a copy of data (7 names today): a brand added later fails S6's pin until the copy is updated.
6. **Shared hot files.** `shards.ts`, `invariants.ts`, `budget.ts`, `caps.ts`, `save-world-adapter.test.ts:26`, `world-flags.ts`, `docs/WORLD.md` are edited by two or three tracks; each hunk is additive and the lead keeps all.
7. **Bundle.** The modules are eager. At the measured 4.1 gzip bytes a source line (P1, X48; UNVERIFIED as a model of R4) the track's 2,890 eager source lines (3,020 less S11's 130 kit and doc lines) are about 12 KB, about a fifth of the shared +60,000 allowance; the lead measures at CP2 and CP3 and a B3 delta above about 30 KB stops B4 until the owner answers.
8. **A kernel nobody calls can rot.** S11's scripted caller and the synthetic protectors keep it exercised until the Life track wires it (L4 is one wave after S8).
9. **Protector order and failure.** Protectors and salience run in id order; one that throws is caught and protects nothing (a test pins it), so a buggy protector cannot stop the sweep or a tap. A record that carries its own stake flags, obligation or grudge is protected without any protector.
10. **Frozen tables nobody reads.** `TRAIT_AXES`, `STAFF_AXES`, the voice vectors, `KIND_AGE` and `FACET` pass their shape tests whatever the numbers are, and `KIND_AGE` freezes at the first golden recording. Mitigation: the lead reads each table at its merge (S3's before the recording; cross-check 3.3, X57).
