# AnotherLife v2.4.0 — Real Markets, Real Shares

Markets now live through history's real booms and busts, you decide how a company's shares are
split and how much of it an IPO sells, and selling out of a family company finally lets it go.

## 📈 Markets that follow real history

Every company in the game lives through the real events in their real years. That means yours,
your rivals', your relatives' and every NPC's. The companies at the center of an event are hit
hard; everyone else feels it more mildly.

| Event | Years | Hit hardest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Great Depression | 1929–34 | banks, investment firms, property, shops, travel |
| The 1973 oil crisis | 1973–75 | fuel-hungry businesses and travel; oil companies cash in |
| The 1979 oil shock | 1979–82 | the same, plus record interest rates for property |
| The Gulf War oil spike | 1990–91 | travel and transport |
| Japan's bubble bursts | 1990–94 | Japanese property, banks and investors |
| The dot-com boom | 1995–99 | internet, software and chip companies soar |
| The Asian financial crisis | 1997–99 | banks and property across East Asia |
| The dot-com crash | 2000–03 | internet, software and chip companies collapse |
| The housing crash and financial crisis | 2006–11 | property, banks and investment firms, worst in the US, UK, Ireland and Spain |
| The euro crisis | 2010–13 | southern European banks and property |
| The oil price collapse | 2014–16 | oil companies (and oil-exporting countries) |
| The 2020 crash and oil collapse | 2020–21 | travel, hotels and going out; online shopping and software boom |
| The 2022 market crash | 2022–23 | tech, investors and property; energy booms |
| The AI boom | 2023–26 | AI labs, chip makers and data centers soar |

- An event changes three things: how many customers a market has, what inputs cost, and how much
  investors pay for every dollar of profit. A software company's value doubles in 1999 and falls
  by more than half in 2001; a property company loses half its value in 2008.
- Listed companies and groups now move with the real stock market in history years, not a random
  draw.
- When an event first hits your companies hard, a news popup explains it and says how each of
  your companies is exposed. The Annual Report and each company's Market & Competition screen
  list the year's events and their effect on that company.
- The game invents no events for the future. After 2026 the AI boom's premium fades gently by
  2030 instead of ending in a crash that never happened.

## 🧾 Your shares, your way

- **Choose the number of shares when you found a company.** A slider runs from 1,000 to 10 billion
  shares (10 million by default). It changes nobody's ownership, only what one share is worth.
  The preview shows the price of a share now and once the company is big enough to list.
- **Change it any time before listing** from Capital & Ownership › Share Count.
- **Retail investors care about the price of one share.** Affordable shares draw crowds of retail
  buyers. Very expensive shares, or penny-stock prices below a dollar, put them off.
- **Pick how much an IPO sells.** Going public now has a slider from 5% to 60% of the company
  (25% as before by default), for companies and whole conglomerates. The preview shows the shares
  on offer, the expected price, how many times orders should cover the offer, the money raised
  and the stake you keep. It warns you if you would keep less than half.
- **Demand sets the result.** An oversubscribed IPO prices up to 15% higher and pops more on day
  one. An undersubscribed one sells only part of its shares, at a discount. Booms make listing
  easier; crashes make it harder.
- Affordable shares also mean more buyers when you sell some later.

## 🐞 Fixes

- **Selling out of a family company now lets it go.** If you sold all your shares of a listed
  company or conglomerate that your siblings also owned, it stayed in your list at "0 yours". Now
  it leaves your companies. Each relative keeps their part as their own wealth, at the market
  price, and the notice says so. Holdings already left at 0% are cleaned up at your next
  birthday.

## Old saves

Companies keep their numbers. Companies without a chosen share count list at about $25 a share,
as before. Market events apply from the next year you play. Nothing is paid or refunded, except
that relatives of a listed company you already sold out of receive their part when it is cleaned
up.
