# AnotherLife v2.6.1: Fair insider cases

A player who was a UK member of parliament put 2 trillion pounds into one company's stock and passed a law that moved its shares. The player was charged with insider trading and faced a base sentence of 40 to 80 years. The regulator took money from the bank and it never came back. There was no suit to bring.

This release fixes each step. For a law, only shares you bought this year or last year count, and you're warned before the vote. A case is charged and sentenced for what it is. The money an investigation takes is held and comes back when you're cleared. An acquittal gives you a real suit to bring, and Do Anything points to it. A big buy order says where price impact starts to cost you before you place it, and what a full sale would return after.

## Insider cases

- **Timing counts for laws.** For a law, a share is an insider position only if you bought it in the current or the previous game year. Shares you've held longer don't count. Open bets and index options count whenever they were placed. Each investment now keeps the units bought in those two years (`details.buys`); a holding saved without that falls back to the year of its first purchase. A sale uses this year's units first, then last year's, then the older ones.
- **Announcements count every holding.** A deliberate Market Mover announcement counts every share you hold, however long you've held it, as it did in v2.6.0.
- **One case per security.** A security has at most one policy case that isn't closed, whether it's open or pending while an office holder's immunity lasts. A later law on the same industry adds to that case instead of starting another. The case keeps the larger stake. While it's open, the new shares' paper gain adds to its gain, and the new bets the law brings are tagged to the case for the investigation to freeze. A pending case has already been investigated, so its gain and held money stay and nothing more is taken. The new bets a law brings to it are frozen at once, and you lose what you staked. The Bill Passed or Order Signed notice adds "Investigators froze your new bets on X right away, since your case there is already open. You lost the Y you staked." Bets placed after that law aren't tagged. While the case is pending, a later law that helps those bets freezes them too, and a Market Mover announcement starts its own case for them.
- **What a later law adds.** A later law that only brings shares adds no heat or karma loss, and doesn't count as a second insider trade. A bet in your own name or an index option moves the case up to an own-bet case and adds 8 heat, once per law. A crony's bet joins the case but doesn't move it. A shares case stays on shares, which the press is likelier to find than a crony's account, and gets no extra heat. A bet or index option raises the evidence to at least its own route's starting level, so a crony's bet leaves a shares case's evidence as it was.
- **New positions restart the quiet years.** An open case goes cold after four years in which the press doesn't find it. A later law that grows the stake or the gain, moves the case up or brings bets starts that count over. A case quiet for three years doesn't go cold the year after a big new position.
- **A fair charge.** A policy case records whether the law helped the company (`bullish`). A law that helped is charged as Abuse of Office, a law that hurt as Market Manipulation. The first law decides it, and a later law doesn't change it. Before this release every policy case was charged as Market Manipulation. A saved case without the field keeps that label.
- **A fair sentence.** A policy case that a law started on held shares alone has severity 3 at most, a base sentence of 5 to 15 years. It used to reach severity 5, a base of 40 to 80 years, for a stake of 10 million dollars or more (scaled to the country's wealth). The cap holds while the case stays on shares, and a crony's bet from a later law doesn't change that. Once a later law brings a bet in your own name or an index option, it's charged as before. Cases on bets or index options, and announcement cases, are unchanged.
- **A warning before the vote.** The bill popup, for a vote and for an executive order, adds a plain line when the law would start a case or change one it adds to. It names the companies and the regulator ("You bought shares in X recently. This moves their price. The FCA could call it insider trading."). With two or more companies it says "their prices". With bets or index options it starts "You hold positions in X." A law that only adds to a case says "This moves X again. It adds to the insider trading case the FCA could bring.", and "cases" when there's more than one. When the law would bring new bets to a pending case, the line adds "Your new bets on X would be frozen right away, and you'd lose what you staked." A law that would change nothing gets no line. Seeing it draws no random numbers and changes nothing.
- **The press story.** When the press finds a case on shares, the popup no longer says an account in your name made a killing betting. For a law, which counts only recent shares, it says "... reports that you bought X (SYM) shares shortly before you ...", and the life log says "The press found out I bought X shares before my own law moved its price." A Market Mover announcement counts a holding of any age, so it says "... reports that you held X (SYM) shares when you ...", and the life log says "The press found out I held X shares when I made my announcement." For a coin there's no "shares". A case on bets keeps the betting story.

## Money held by an investigation

- When the regulator opens a case, the cash it takes is held on the case (`held`) instead of being destroyed. The scandal notice says the investigators are holding it until the case is decided. Open bets they freeze are still lost, as before. A case never holds more than the profit the regulator claims, however many times it's opened.
- The arrest carries the money and the regulator's name to the trial, for the immediate arrest and for the later arrest of a case that waited out an office holder's immunity.
- **Cleared:** an acquittal pays it back once ("You got back the X taken in your case."). An appeal that overturns the conviction, or a death-row exoneration, pays it back once from the conviction record. The Appeal Won and Exonerated notices name the money last, after where you'll live.
- **Conviction or plea:** the court keeps it, and now says so. The verdict ends "The court kept the X taken in your case. Winning an appeal returns it." A death sentence ends "Going free on appeal returns it." instead, since a commuted sentence returns nothing. The plea offer adds "Pleading guilty means the court keeps the X taken in your case."
- **Appeal:** while the sentence's one appeal is still to file, the Appeal menu adds "A win returns the X taken in your case." Once it's used, the menu says "The court keeps the X taken in your case." On death row, where you can appeal once a year, it says "If you go free, you get back the X taken in your case." A commuted death sentence returns nothing. Off death row, a lost appeal, or one that only cuts the sentence, ends "The court keeps the X taken in your case."
- **Also kept:** running from the arrest, bribing the officer, an informant deal, a pardon, parole and serving the time all keep it. An arrest while you're already in prison is a conviction with no trial. Its notice says the court kept the money and promises no appeal.
- **No trial:** a case that closes without an arrest pays back what it held and says so in the life log. That covers the statute of limitations and a case file "lost in a flood". A case that stays pending keeps it.
- If the character dies with money held, it isn't returned.

## Suing

- An acquittal that follows a false accusation, or a prosecution by a named agency, files a real suit in Activities, Lawsuit. The notice says "You can sue the FCA under Activities, Lawsuit." exactly when one was filed. The row shows the agency's name; prices, fees, odds and damages are unchanged. A minor gets none (Lawsuit is 18 and over), and neither does an acquittal that sends a fugitive back to prison.

## Do Anything

- Legal wording ("sue the FCA", "fight the seizure in court", "hire a lawyer", "legal action") gets a pointer to Activities, Lawsuit. Under 18 it gives the age rule. The answer changes no money, stat or record and draws no random numbers.
- **Appeals.** An appeal is legal wording when it's about a case: "appeal my conviction", "file an appeal", "lodge an appeal", "win my appeal", "start an appeal against the verdict". Launching, starting or making an appeal with no court decision in the sentence is a fundraiser, so "launch an appeal for the flood victims" and "make an appeal for donations" keep the rule they had in v2.6.0.
- **In prison,** talk of the conviction ("appeal my conviction", "fight the charges", "appeal the verdict") or of a lawyer with no suit in it ("hire a lawyer", "lawyer up") points at Appeal in the Prison menu. When the appeal is used up, it gives the Appeal option's own reason. A minor in juvenile detention gets the same answer. Suing from a cell still says Lawsuit is closed while you're locked up. Outside prison the same words get the Lawsuit pointer, or the age rule.
- **Money held in your case, asked for from a cell.** When the case behind your sentence holds money and you ask for it back ("sue the FCA to get my money back", "hire a lawyer to get my money back", "fight the seizure in court"), the answer says "Winning an appeal returns the X taken in your case. (Prison menu, Appeal.)". On death row it says going free on appeal returns it. Once the sentence's appeal is used, it says the court keeps it. Under 18, a suit still gets the age rule.
- **Other rules keep their sentences.** A crime at the start, or joined on with "and", "then" or a comma, still runs the crime ("sue him and rob him" steals). Six rules can share a sentence with a court or a lawyer: proposing, quitting, breaking up, gambling, drugs and drinking. A court or a lawyer that only sets the scene leaves the sentence to one of them ("hire a divorce lawyer" is a breakup, "get married in the courthouse" a proposal). A court next to a wedding, a marriage or a divorce isn't read as a suit either. When the sentence sues or appeals, one of those six keeps it only when its action has a clause of its own, joined by "and", "then", "plus", a comma or a semicolon ("quit my job and sue my boss" quits), or when the suit is "for divorce" ("sue my wife for divorce" is a breakup). Any other action stays part of the suit ("sue the casino" and "sue them and go shopping" are suits).
- **A person called Sue.** A lowercase "sue" after a verb like "kiss" or "call", or after any "with", is read as a name, and so is one after "and" at the very end ("go bowling with sue", "go to the movies with tom and sue"). Those keep the rule they had in v2.6.0. A capital "Sue" is the verb at the start or after "want to" or "I'll" ("I want to Sue the FCA"). "sueing" counts, and a leading "go" or "please" doesn't change the verb.

## Big orders

- **Before:** the Buy row for a listed stock, a penny stock or a coin names the size where price impact starts to cost more than 10% ("Orders above about X lose over 10% to price impact"). The amount dialog says the same and opens on a round amount under that size when a quarter of your cash would go past it. The 10% counts price impact only, not the broker's fee. In a US game the row for an ordinary listed stock says about $192M before your own buying has moved the price, and with $3 trillion in cash the dialog opens at $190,000,000.
- **After:** when an order loses more than 10% to price impact, the Order Filled notice says so ("The order moved the price, so on paper it lost 99.7% of what you paid."). An order under that mark keeps the old line about order-book depth.
- **Marked and sale value:** when the position's marked value and what selling it all would return are more than 5% apart, the notice gives each once, with the sale last ("Your position is valued at X. A full sale would return about Y, before tax."). A deposit says "Closing it now would return about Y". Y is the figure the Sell All row shows. It's the header's rule, so a top-up of a lifted position gets it too, and so does a deposit or a tiny order that a penalty or a fee cuts by more than 5%. Otherwise the notice ends "Your position is now worth X." as before.
- **The header:** when selling the whole portfolio would pay more than 5% above or below its marked value, the Investments header adds "Sells for about X". The marked figures stay as they were.
- The savings notice reads "into savings" (a term deposit reads "into a term deposit").
- A 2 trillion dollar order in one listed stock is marked at 0.3% of what was paid, and selling it all returns about 98.6%. Price impact, fees, taxes and valuations are unchanged.

## Old saves

- Every new field is optional and checked when a game loads. `held` on an insider case must be a finite number of 0 or more, is rounded, and is capped at 1e15 per case; anything else is dropped. A case saved before this release holds nothing, so one that already took money under the old rules isn't paid back. `bullish` is kept only when it's true or false. `refund` on a crime record is dropped when it's negative or not a number. `details.buys` on an investment is read only when it's a plain object, and never counts more units than the holding has.
- The save schema number stays 2.

## Not in this release

- A partial sale of a position your own buying lifted is taxed on a gain that isn't there, and the sell fee is charged on the lifted value. Both are left as they were.
- A case's stake and paper gain are still worked out from the marked value of the shares, not from what a sale would return.
- Two life-log lines still say "announcement" after a law. A case that goes cold reads "Nobody ever connected my X bet to my announcement.", which also says "bet" for shares. The press finding a bet reads "before my announcement". A Market Mover announcement on shares still says "The SYM you quietly bought beforehand is suddenly worth a lot more.", however long you've held them.
- Do Anything: "press charges" and "report him to the police" still get the generic answer, and a murder or self-harm wording still runs its own rule first. A sentence whose only legal word is a final "and sue" ("gather evidence and sue") reads it as the name, as v2.6.0 did, and "me and sue go bowling" still reads "sue" as the verb. "file an appeal for the charity" and "win an appeal for the charity" are read as legal wording.
