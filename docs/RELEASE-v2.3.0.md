# AnotherLife v2.3.0 — Products, R&D and Buy-outs 2.0

Products now compete head to head with your rivals' versions, every kind of product follows its
real history, R&D costs belong to the product instead of your bank balance, and buying a rival
finally brings its products with it.

## 🧪 R&D: every idea has its own price

- **Fixed development costs.** An idea costs what developing that product costs in your trade
  and city, the same for a corner shop and a giant. Your company's cash and sales no longer move
  the slider: it always runs from half to four times the idea's cost. Cash only decides whether
  you can pay, and the preview says how much is missing.
- **Money buys quality.** A bigger budget is likelier to work, quicker and makes a better line,
  with gains that flatten past about twice the normal budget. The preview shows the quality you
  can expect against the rivals' versions and how many more customers it should bring.
- **Team slots.** A team runs a few projects at once without strain (more for a bigger company);
  more projects than that stretch it.
- Launches land within a few points of the quoted quality (a smash hit adds ten), and the results
  say how each new line stands against the rivals.

## 🥊 Products win customers from rivals

- Every line has its own quality. Rivals take up each kind of product once it catches on (big
  rivals first), each with its own version. The better your line is than theirs, the more of their
  customers switch to you; a kind few rivals sell yet pulls extra, so early movers win.
- Rivals with worse versions lose those customers first; rivals with better ones keep theirs.
  Each line on sale shows its quality against the rivals', where its demand is heading, the share
  of your customers it brings and whose customers it wins.
- Lines age: rivals improve their versions every year (fastest in tech), so yesterday's hit needs
  a next generation.

## 📈 Real historical demand, stacking

- Every product the game offers has a demand curve from real history: when it was first sold,
  when it went mainstream, how big its market is and when it faded. Street lighting spreads from
  the 1880s, coal plants peak around 2008, house calls fade after the 1930s, cronut knockoffs last
  about a year, and products tied to a technology (a smartphone chip, a fitness app) follow its
  adoption. Poorer countries adopt later.
- Different kinds of product add up one for one; variants of one kind (premium, budget, regional
  editions, bundles, add-ons, next generations) share its customers. Ideas only appear once their
  kind exists in your market, and trades that lacked them get period classics (telephone
  exchanges and telegraph service before fiber broadband).

## 🤝 Buying a rival brings its products and whatnot

- A buy-out now hands over the rival's product lines you don't sell yet, at its quality, and
  replaces your own version of a kind when its version is better.
- Its sites keep trading across your country's biggest cities instead of piling into one.
- Its people's know-how lifts your quality when theirs was better, and its name adds some brand.
- The buy-out row, the confirmation and the notice list what comes with it; every rival profile
  lists what the rival sells.

## 🐞 Fixes

- Money sliders that open on a suggestion start on a round amount in your currency, and their
  notches stay round in currencies worth more than a dollar (no more "441326.96" in the exact
  amount field).

## Old saves

Product lines from older saves get a quality from their old demand boost and keep selling;
projects in development keep their budget, odds and timing. Nothing is paid or refunded. Lines
now retire automatically after 30 years (rivals leave them behind long before).
