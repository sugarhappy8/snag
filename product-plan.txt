SNAGDEALS — PRODUCT PLAN
Unified Wishlist + Price Tracking + Smart Buy Automation

==================================================
1. PRODUCT SUMMARY
==================================================

SnagDeals is a consumer SaaS platform that lets users track products they want across multiple stores and platforms, define the price they are willing to pay, and get notified—or eventually have the product purchased automatically—when that price condition is met.

The first version should focus on gaming.

Initial supported ecosystems:
- Steam
- GOG
- Epic Games Store
- PlayStation Store
- Xbox Store

Later expansion:
- Amazon
- Best Buy
- Walmart
- Newegg
- eBay
- Manufacturer stores
- Other online retailers

Core idea:

"One wishlist for everything you want, with price targets and smart purchase rules."

The long-term concept is similar to a limit order in financial markets:

"I want this product, but only if it reaches this price."

Instead of repeatedly checking stores, the user sets a buy condition once and SnagDeals monitors the market for them.

==================================================
2. THE PROBLEM
==================================================

Consumers currently have to deal with separate wishlists across many platforms.

A gamer may have:
- 20 games on Steam
- 10 games on PlayStation
- 5 games on Xbox
- 6 games on GOG
- subscriptions such as Game Pass or PlayStation Plus

The problems are:

1. Wishlists are fragmented.
2. Users miss good discounts.
3. Users often buy too early.
4. Discount percentages can be misleading.
5. Users do not know whether a current price is actually good.
6. People may accidentally buy something already included in a subscription.
7. People may buy the same product twice on different platforms.
8. Users have to manually keep checking different stores.
9. Existing trackers usually stop at alerts.
10. There is no simple consumer system for setting automated purchasing rules across stores.

SnagDeals solves these problems with one unified purchasing dashboard.

==================================================
3. CORE VALUE PROPOSITION
==================================================

SnagDeals tells the user:

- what they want

The user should feel like:

"I tell SnagDeals what I want once, and I never have to monitor it again."

==================================================

Available rules:

==================================================
10. AUTO-BUY
==================================================

Auto-buy is the most differentiated long-term feature.

However, it should NOT be included universally in the first version.

The system should have three levels:

LEVEL 1 — ALERT
SnagDeals detects the target price and notifies the user.

LEVEL 2 — ONE-CLICK BUY
SnagDeals opens the correct store/product page so the user can complete checkout.

LEVEL 3 — AUTO-BUY
SnagDeals completes the purchase automatically only where official merchant integrations, checkout APIs, approved payment flows, or partnerships make this safe and allowed.

Important rule:

Do not build the business around storing users' Steam, PlayStation, Xbox, Amazon, or other retailer passwords.

Avoid:
- browser bots
- credential storage
- scraping private accounts
- bypassing 2FA
- bypassing CAPTCHAs
- violating merchant terms

Auto-buy should be introduced store by store only where technically and legally supported.

11. HISTORICAL PRICE INTELLIGENCE
==================================================

Every product should display price history.

The system should answer:

"Is this deal actually good?"

Data shown:
- current price
- full price
- current discount
- historical low
- historical low date
- typical discounted price
- average sale price
- frequency of discounts
- price trend

Possible labels:
- Poor deal
- Normal price
- Good deal
- Excellent deal
- Near historical low
- New historical low

Avoid relying only on advertised percentage-off values.

A 50% discount from a fake or inflated MSRP may still be a bad deal.

==================================================

SnagDeals should never encourage the user to buy more.

Its purpose is to help users buy what they already want:

- cheaper
- at the right time
- without duplicate purchases
- without repeatedly checking stores
- without missing major discounts
